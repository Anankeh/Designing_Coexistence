# Designing coexistence: the zoo that fixed a neighborhood

A behavioral design case study. A community of 528 homes across six buildings, with residents of many nationalities, moved from complaint management toward self-regulation through social norms, culturally familiar characters, and the Friendly Compliance method. Conduct improved by a reported 73 percent.

**Open it:** [anankeh.github.io/Designing_Coexistence](https://anankeh.github.io/Designing_Coexistence/)

## The story in four steps

1. **The problem.** Six buildings, one quiet rule: every family for itself. House rules reached people unevenly, and the administration answered complaints one at a time.
2. **The intervention.** A cast of animal characters, each one already part of the residents' everyday vocabulary, carried the rules with humor and sympathy instead of penalties.
3. **The language.** Every character was chosen for what it already meant to the people who would meet it.
4. **The result.** A community that began to regulate itself.

## The method

Friendly Compliance changes behavior by lowering defensiveness rather than adding force. It applies wherever people resist rules they did not write, from neighborhoods to security awareness programs.
